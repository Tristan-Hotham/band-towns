## Summary of the contribution (150 words)

Open, crowd-sourced archives are attractive sources for the geography of live music, but their coverage is rarely audited. I place every UK event in MusicBrainz in its local authority and model the 7,615 documented concerts of 1960 to 2025 with a pre-specified plan. The record is thin, a median of 120 concerts a year since 1980, and lumpy, shaped by fan-documented tours and a few venue archives. Documented concerts rise steeply with population (elasticity 1.64). Areas with a 10,000+ seat arena have 3.8 times the documented concerts of comparable areas, a premium concentrated in tour dates and stronger than placebo arenas; the 14 arena areas hold 10% of people and 53% of tour dates. The expected arena shadow is not found. 70 of 167 large areas have no documented tour date. The paper argues that such data map attention to music, and offers a coverage audit.
